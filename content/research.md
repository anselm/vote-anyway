# Research & sources

The essay makes an argument for more accessible participation. Its appendices carry the evidence, objections, prior work and questions that the experiment needs to confront.

## The essay’s companion appendices

- [Ways of deciding together](/appendix-collective-decisions.html) — the history and purposes of voting methods: choosing a winner, representing a community and finding common ground. Includes limited voting, cumulative voting, STV and worked examples, building on the Nonprofit VOTE article.

- [Security, verification, and field experience](/appendix-security.html) — election properties, cryptographic protocols, remote-voting risks and experience in Estonia and Switzerland. Includes ElectionGuard, Helios and the National Academies’ work.
- [Democratic mechanisms and precedents](/appendix-democracy.html) — deliberation, delegation, participation incentives and counting rules, with references to Smartocracy, liquid democracy, Pol.is, Decidim and vTaiwan.
- [The prototype and its research questions](/appendix-prototype.html) — the experiment’s design and technical questions. For the current implementation, see [Where we are](/about#where-we-are).

## Building on other people’s work

The democracy appendix credits **Brad deGraf** and his Smartocracy coauthors: Marko A. Rodriguez, Daniel J. Steinbock, Jennifer H. Watkins, Carlos Gershenson, Johan Bollen and Victor Grey. Their [2007 Smartocracy paper](https://library.uniteddiversity.coop/Decision_Making_and_Democracy/Smartocracy.pdf) is a foundation for the discussion of trust networks and delegation. The appendix also links [deGraf’s Planetwork profile](https://www.planetwork.net/people/brad-degraf) and related research.

These references are part of the argument: work to learn from, test against, and credit. The security and democracy appendices retain their source links alongside the claims they support.

## Voting methods: the next experiment

[Electing someone, without losing the second choice](/voting-methods.html) explains single-winner instant runoffs, compares alternatives, and sets out the proposed next feature. It includes a worked example, official guidance and research on the method’s limitations.
