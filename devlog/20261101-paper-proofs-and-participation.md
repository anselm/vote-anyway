# November 1, 2026 — Paper, proofs and participation

The essay now brings together the project's original argument, Anselm's [Substack introduction](https://anselm.substack.com/p/vote-anyway), and questions raised in a discussion with the OpenVTC community.

The central question remains: why not also vote digitally? The revision carries forward the urgency of finding ways to decide together, the ambition for participation as public fabric, and a perspective grounded in the experience of the person trying to vote.

Separate sections make the arguments for and against paper voting. Paper provides evidence outside the counting software; physical participation and custody also have costs. The essay distinguishes ordinary electronic machines, cryptographically verifiable elections, and remote voting from personal devices. The security appendix adds sources on software independence, zero-knowledge proofs, Helios and hybrid designs. Zero-knowledge proofs have a long history; the opportunity lies in continued research and usable implementations.

A new [community-voting appendix](/appendix-community-voting.html) examines the capabilities described by OpenVTC's developer: eligibility tickets, question formats, registration and voting periods, recasting, secrecy, result visibility and a separate ballot-submission service. It treats them as reported capabilities, not independently verified security guarantees. It also distinguishes OpenVTC's infrastructure from Vote Anyway's existing attest.monster integration.

The working prototype still supports single-choice topics, encrypted submissions, receipts and signed records. This is a writing and research update, with no changes to authentication, ballot handling, counting or database structure. Ranked voting and privacy-preserving eligibility remain further work.

The current essay is maintained separately from the preserved first draft. Earlier writing and all existing appendices remain available; the longer Substack article is also retained in the repository's writing archive. The website is the continuing home for the fuller argument, with a shorter Substack introduction prepared to point readers here.
