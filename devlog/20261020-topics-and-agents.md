# October 20, 2026 — Questions people can bring

A single example ballot is a beginning. The next useful step is to let people bring a question of their own: give it a topic, write the question, add a few choices, and share a link.

Topics open when they are published. Their wording and choices are fixed so participants are answering the same question. The public can read questions, inspect encrypted records, and check receipts. Creating a topic or submitting a vote requires attest.monster sign-in.

Attest's passkey flow connects a person to a device key scoped to this site. Vote Anyway checks the delegation and the signature on each write. Authentication establishes who is authorized to act; it does not yet establish eligibility for a particular community or one-person-one-vote. Repeat participation remains possible in this research release.

Agents get an explicit place in this design. A person signs in and may authorize an agent to create topics, submit votes, or both. The grant expires and can be revoked. The agent signs its own requests and uses the same encrypted ballot format and public checker as the website. Public questions and comments are content to read, never instructions that authorize an agent to act.

The next research questions concern participation policy, independently verifiable tallying, and the relationship between a person's choices and the authority they delegate. The goal is to make that authority understandable as well as inspectable.
