# Vote Anyway

*Why not also vote digitally?*

![Early voters at the Midtown Mall in Anchorage. Photo: Yereth Rosen / Alaska Beacon.](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/cd1840d6-4e3c-4771-9904-f66a858b93e7_800x534.jpeg)

Early voters line up at the Midtown Mall in Anchorage on Thursday, Oct. 22, 2026. Photo: Yereth Rosen / Alaska Beacon.

*In Flanders fields the poppies glow, row upon row upon row upon row. We are the dead. Short days ago - we lived, felt dawn, saw sunset grow; now find commonality with the foe; break free of demagogues the monsters who would upon the fire our lives throw; live and love and this world know; the torch of freedom be yours to hold up high - if ye break faith together we die. There is nothing noble about a field whose bodies sow, a metaphor of poppies, proxies for immeasurable lives unlived, growing row upon row upon row upon row.*

Humans are very good at dying. We die in masses; Hobbesian atrocities that contradict the stories we tell ourselves about who we are, we divide, we slaughter by the millions. Children go to war, the possibility of their lives slashed. Others starve, denied basic food, safety - a life of labors promises ground upon a tread of hysterical power, crushing us beneath its wheel.

When we are, standing in the ruins of all that we built, forced to be wiser, we do try to identify stakeholders, find common ground, and build tools for rough agreement on how to steer our collective energy. Each movement of peace is hard earned. ‘Never again’ we say as we bury our beloved. Yet all too soon comes a call for freedom, freedom from rules, from the misbehavior of others. We are bad at distinguishing between ‘stretching’ and ‘tearing’ - when society actually ‘tears’ - when the center does not hold, unleash the dogs of war. We cannot distinguish between freedom and fracture. There is no materials science of our collective social economic fabric; no psychohistory of the future.

Are we human or animal? Hobbes or Rousseau? Who gets mad at the ocean when mighty waves knock down their home? Who gets mad at themselves for being so very very dumb about how they build? What emperor declares war on the sea?

Voting is one small part of that larger task. It gives us somewhere concrete to begin.

Look at a photograph of people standing in a long line to vote. We are supposed to feel proud. But what about the people missing? Who couldn’t leave work, find childcare, or stand that long? Participation should not require a demonstration of endurance.

I’ve been building Vote Anyway: an argument for digital voting, alongside a small working experiment. My question is what we could make possible if participation were easier, more frequent, and something people could check for themselves.

I want systems with no center. Where participation is in the fabric, like air. Democracy cannot be owned by a few; anything we build has to be examined—this has to be public fabric.

The case for paper deserves a serious hearing. So does the possibility of cryptographic voting, and of useful tools for schools, associations, neighborhoods and open-source communities. My argument is that we should try harder to explore that space.

I’ve brought the fuller argument, the objections, the research and the experiment together on the project’s website. That is where the essay will continue to develop.

[Read Vote Anyway](https://vote-anyway-728854538261.us-west1.run.app/essay.html) — including the case for and against paper, what cryptographic proofs can change, and what remains to be solved.

[Try the experiment](https://vote-anyway-728854538261.us-west1.run.app/). Build. Inspect. Critique. Learn. Improve.

## The Second Coming

Turning and turning in the widening gyre\
The falcon cannot hear the falconer;\
Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold;\
Mere anarchy is loosed upon the world,\
The blood-dimmed tide is loosed, and everywhere\
The ceremony of innocence is drowned;\
The best lack all conviction, while the worst\
Are full of passionate intensity.\

Surely some revelation is at hand;\
Surely the Second Coming is at hand.\
The Second Coming! Hardly are those words out\
When a vast image out of Spiritus Mundi\
Troubles my sight: somewhere in sands of the desert\
A shape with lion body and the head of a man,\
A gaze blank and pitiless as the sun,\
Is moving its slow thighs, while all about it\
Reel shadows of the indignant desert birds.\
The darkness drops again; but now I know\
That twenty centuries of stony sleep\
Were vexed to nightmare by a rocking cradle,\
And what rough beast, its hour come round at last,\
Slouches towards Bethlehem to be born?\

[William Butler Yeats](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/william-butler-yeats)
