# October 20, 2026 — A wider repertoire of democratic decisions

Anselm brought in Nonprofit VOTE’s [Fair Vote Methods for Multi-Seat Elections](https://www.nonprofitvote.org/fair-vote-methods-for-multi-seat-elections/), widening the discussion beyond electing a single person through a runoff.

The new appendix, [Ways of deciding together](/appendix-collective-decisions.html), distinguishes choosing one winner, representing different constituencies, allocating resources, finding common ground and delegating authority. It introduces historical contributions, limited and cumulative voting, and single transferable vote. Two fictional examples show a majority sweeping a block-vote election and how surplus transfers work in a multi-seat ranked count.

The appendix links the article, scholarly history, electoral administrators, counting rules and the Polis project. It distinguishes advocates’ explanations from operational specifications and avoids promising that a counting method will eliminate disagreement or every strategic incentive.

The direction for the interface is to begin with the purpose: what are people trying to decide together? The proposed first ranked experiment remains a single-winner runoff. This research addition does not implement a new voting method; it gives that experiment a broader context.

The essay and three original companion appendix sources remain unchanged. The new appendix is linked from the research index, reading-page footers and runoff note. The working outline and its eleven appendix sections remain available in the devlog.
