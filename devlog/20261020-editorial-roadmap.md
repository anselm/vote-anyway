# Vote Anyway — editorial and project roadmap

**Title:** Vote Anyway (essay and website)

**Editorial contract:** Keep the main essay readable and short (~2,000 words); place technical explanations, source links, contradictory evidence, previous research, implementation decisions, and detailed policy comparisons in appendices. Update the essay and code together as testing produces new knowledge.

## Narrative

1. **Moral motivation:** legitimate participation can be obstructed even when accepted ballots are perfectly counted. Distinguish procedural access from ballot medium, deliberate suppression from access failures, and security from participation.
2. **Technology half:** the strong capabilities of internet transaction systems; what a secret ballot requires in addition; paper's real audit strength and physical failure modes; the cryptographic verification promise; honest limits of an at-home phone.
3. **Praxis bridge:** an explicitly unofficial parallel-voting laboratory, beginning with a fictional question and eventually mirroring sourced real-ballot questions under strict non-affiliation notices.
4. **Implications half:** lower participation friction, potentially more frequent/scoped/reversible decisions, subject-specific delegation and Smartocracy, participation incentives, and alternative counting rules.
5. **Conclusion:** build, inspect, attack, measure, improve; don't confuse better voting infrastructure with a complete redesign of governance.

## Editorial questions for future iterations

- Does the opening convey urgency about access without misdiagnosing an incident or imputing motives?
- Are the examples of digital transaction systems fair analogies rather than proofs about secret ballots?
- Does the verification discussion distinguish inclusion, intent, and tally correctness clearly?
- Does Smartocracy get accurate attribution to Brad deGraf as coauthor alongside the other early contributors?
- Does the praxis section accurately state what the running prototype can and cannot verify?
- Which improvements should be funded by operational work, and which await reviewed research protocols?

## Publication conditions

Do not label a demo a real election. Do not imply affiliation with election offices, claim representative polling results, or compare an opt-in experimental tally to an official count as evidence of wrongdoing. For actual ballot questions, cite the exact official source, jurisdiction, and date/version.
