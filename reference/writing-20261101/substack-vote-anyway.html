War

In Flanders fields the poppies glow, row upon row upon row upon row. We are the dead. Short days ago - we lived, felt dawn, saw sunset grow; now find commonality with the foe; break free of demagogues the monsters who would upon the fire our lives throw; live and love and this world know; the torch of freedom be yours to hold up high - if ye break faith together we die. There is nothing noble about a field whose bodies sow, a metaphor of poppies, proxies for immeasurable lives unlived, growing row upon row upon row upon row.

Humans are very good at dying. We die in masses; Hobbesian atrocities that contradict the stories we tell ourselves about who we are, we divide, we slaughter by the millions. Children go to war, the possibility of their lives slashed. Others starve, denied basic food, safety - a life of labors promises ground upon a tread of hysterical power, crushing us beneath its wheel.

When we are, standing in the ruins of all that we built, forced to be wiser, we do try to identify stakeholders, find common ground, and build tools for rough agreement on how to steer our collective energy. Each movement of peace is hard earned. ‘Never again’ we say as we bury our beloved. Yet all too soon comes a call for freedom, freedom from rules, from the misbehavior of others. We are bad at distinguishing between ‘stretching’ and ‘tearing’ - when society actually ‘tears’ - when the center does not hold, unleash the dogs of war. We cannot distinguish between freedom and fracture. There is no materials science of our collective social economic fabric; no psychohistory of the future.

There are consequences when we can’t make decisions together. We play with fire, fail to appreciate how fragile these systems we built are; built on promises and lies. Failure to reach a rough alignment or consensus. Badly built conceits. A life-altering vote for Brexit balanced on a 50.1 percent plurality. Malevolent politicians hewing at the roots crying ‘voter fraud! voter fraud!’; dragging the physics of our reality itself into question. Rent asunder in a world where we now have nukes.

Are we human or animal? Hobbes or Rousseau? Who gets mad at the ocean when mighty waves knock down their home? Who gets mad at themselves for being so very very dumb about how they build? What emperor declares war on the sea?

Voting

There is of course a rich history of ways to reach collective decisions - we’ve tried them all: fascism and tyranny comes to mind, splitting the tribe, cage matches, wars, cybernetics, or even voting itself - ranked ballots, proportional representation, cumulative voting, deliberation, delegation.

If we restrict this conversation merely to ‘voting’ - the small part of reality we maybe can have some impact on - then we can ask other questions. Covering the basics, security, safety, ease of use is only part of the question. What should voting let us do? What can improving voting let us do that we may not appreciate?

Let’s start simple. Witness a photograph of people standing in a long line to vote. Are we supposed to feel proud? Civic participation - civic pride! I Voted! I’m a good person! A blue ink smudge, a sticker. What about the people missing? Who was not counted? Who couldn’t leave work? Who couldn’t find childcare? Who was handicapped? Who couldn’t stand that long? Participation should not require a demonstration of endurance. And that’s just the very tip of a Titanic aspiration broken upon a floe.

Delegating power to a person is a problem; piled on top of the original crisis. Choosing a representative council or deciding how to spend a neighborhood budget - now you have birthed a small monster - that someday you must slaughter in some transparent abbotoir. Rolling over power becomes a question; how we delegate and how long that delegation lasts, and if that person so empowered is restricted from further corruption after a certain point. Voting feels like ‘a way’ to reach consensus - to basically steal the energy of the few to support an overall direction of the many - but the machinery itself is non-trivial. How do we avoid some of the catastrophic cases where voters vote away their freedom to vote?

Voting is expensive. We must vote more often, more directly, on smaller issues, with trust, verifiability, protections against duress - but the physical cost is too high.

Voting fundamentally is an IQ test - it tests that one is of age, can parse a document, can mark up a page. It is a filter - often on western habits, on language, on specific skills. Not all stakeholders participate.

Voting is design. Good design is hard. Human scale design, design that isn’t too loud, too fast, too abrasive, too hard to read, that avoids say using shampoo bottle or restaurant menu font and color choices (usually dark brown backgrounds with tiny black font that is completely illegible to anybody over the age of 12), that doesn’t rush people, that understands the ‘surround’ the larger context of the venue, maybe even the weather, that humans have frailties.

Voting is political. The Smartmatic voting system in Los Angeles was built by a bunch of Venezuelans who had to escape when Maduro told them what the vote outcome he would prefer was. It’s not only a good example of how bad actors try so hard to distort the fabric but also a good example of just how many design challenges had to be examined to build a new piece of voting hardware.

Here arrives the argument for digital voting - not in opposition to paper - but as well. We frankly can’t argue against paper - the polemic is strong with this one - but we can argue that digital can participate.

How do we fight the thesis that paper voting is the only way to vote? This isn’t actually about being digital but rather that we must build ‘systems with no center’. Where the fabric itself supports voting. And not ‘voting’ exactly but simply sentiment testing. The question is how to make that argument? How does one argue that digital voting should also be a tool? That the internet can be secure, and that new possibilities open up if we do this?

We do have world-scale internet machines - we have seen DAO’s and other attempts at digital voting. I argue this deserves more study. We need to push basic systems of humanity away from being something we ourselves can distort, we must enfranchise stakeholders who don’t meet our capricious IQ tests, we must rollover power, we must do so many things. Voting online is really just the beginning of a much longer search for islands of stability.

Praxis

Let’s save the world - lets fix voting to begin with.

The site is an essay, research appendices, and an experiment. You can sign in, create a question, share it, and submit a ballot. The ballot is encrypted in your browser. You receive a receipt that lets you check whether it appears in the published record, and the tools for inspecting that record are available too.

In this code sketch there is much, if not most, left to solve. This prototype does not establish voter eligibility or one person, one vote. The prototype is not distributed - it runs on one computer. Receipts help check inclusion; but do not prove that the final tally is correct. The first version supports simple, single-choice topics. Ranked voting and runoff methods are among the next things to explore.

I’ve vibed up appendices as a kind of defense against the basic insults to any idea - to connect this work to people who have been thinking about these questions for longer than the few million tokens I threw at it with ChatGPT. I am not sure anybody reads anything any more at all. But I feel like at least one has to pretend. All I can say is today we can speak with code, we can argue a thesis with a working example. Building small lets us see what is needed for scale.

Redux: Why not also vote digitally?

Personally, honestly, I’m more environmentalist than humanist, more capitalist than communist, more interested in humans as change agents in a larger ecosystem of life than necessarily only human happiness (the best way for humans to be happy is to protect the world they live in). Within this field of people, our passions, our concerns, I am however interested in ‘democracy’ becoming something we can practice more often: in associations, neighborhoods, cooperatives, and public life. Democracy cannot be owned by a few; anything we build has to be examined - this has to be public fabric.

What is happening now is madness. Our politicians divide us, driving us towards their ends, and we rush scattered, failing to challenge the premise of these vanishing few. Are we trending towards war? Or do we just project perennial anxieties?

Here in British Columbia, as I write this, a voting phenom just happened, almost like the natural phenomena it should really be, and with a sharp division between local and provincial values. In the United States sharp divisions are also being magnified by prancing demons. In Brazil we saw surprising outcomes - with the opposing party actually fleeing the country (it is curious when a party has to flee if they don’t win!). Britain seems to now rue how it engaged its decisions. France seems to be undergoing some identity paroxysm. Israel itself may shift away from hardline positions. Russia had a pretend vote for reasons that seem unclear. Around the world this seems to be more and more urgent to ask: what if voting was in the fabric, like air, not run by ‘anybody’, but by the people for the people, a measure of their own sentiment - uninterruptible, uninterferable, a poll at least of real will - not necessarily about decisions per se.

Talk is cheap. I am sure there are thousands of people who will happily rip any digital voting thesis to shreds; who will say “paper is sacrosanct!” “you don’t know anything” “stay in your lane!”. Others will say “oh I have a much better digital voting solution!”. People are so happy to critique without their own solution. Others will say “first you must read these 5 large papers”. I don’t know how to respond to all that with words.

Praxis feels necessary. As a programmer, my instinct is to move between argument and experiment. Build. Inspect. Critique. Learn. Improve. We should take both security and participation seriously enough to keep trying. This whole thing I put together, this essay, this code - it is not intended to be an ‘answer’ - it’s really just a question: Why not also vote digitally?

Here were some words. And here is Vote Anyway, read more words,try the experiment.

The Second Coming



Turning and turning in the widening gyre

The falcon cannot hear the falconer;

Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold;

Mere anarchy is loosed upon the world,

The blood-dimmed tide is loosed, and everywhere

The ceremony of innocence is drowned;

The best lack all conviction, while the worst

Are full of passionate intensity.

Surely some revelation is at hand;

Surely the Second Coming is at hand.

The Second Coming! Hardly are those words out

When a vast image out of Spiritus Mundi

Troubles my sight: somewhere in sands of the desert

A shape with lion body and the head of a man,

A gaze blank and pitiless as the sun,

Is moving its slow thighs, while all about it

Reel shadows of the indignant desert birds.

The darkness drops again; but now I know

That twenty centuries of stony sleep

Were vexed to nightmare by a rocking cradle,

And what rough beast, its hour come round at last,

Slouches towards Bethlehem to be born?

William Butler Yeats
